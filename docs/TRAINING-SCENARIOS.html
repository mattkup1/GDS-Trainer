Practice Scenarios

Five booking exercises, beginner to advanced — try each one yourself before checking the solution

Open ../web/index.html in a browser (or run the gds-trainer command-line edition) and sign in with SI. Each scenario below gives you a task and the specific flight/passenger details to use — flight data in this simulator is deterministic (seeded by route, date, and flight number), so entering the exact same details shown here will reproduce the exact same flights, seat maps, and fare quotes as the solutions at the end of this document. Work through each scenario on your own first; the Solutions section starting on the next page has the full command stream and a line-by-line explanation for every scenario. See HOW-TO-BOOK-A-FLIGHT.md or GDS-TRAINER-BOOKING-GUIDE.html/.pdf for command syntax if you get stuck.

1

A simple one-way ticket

Beginner

A customer, John Smith, wants to fly from Dallas/Ft. Worth (DFW) to Chicago O'Hare (ORD) on August 15, 2026. He wants the earliest flight of the day and doesn't care about class of service — economy (Y) is fine. He'll pay cash and needs a ticket issued today.

  • Search availability for the route/date above and sell 1 seat on the earliest departing flight.
  • Add his name, price the itinerary, and add a contact phone, received-from, ticketing arrangement, and form of payment.
  • Save the PNR and issue his ticket.

New ground covered: sign-in, availability, sell, name field, pricing, phone/RF/ticketing/FOP, end transaction, ticketing.

2

A family with an infant and special requests

Beginner+

John Smith and Jane Smith are flying from Los Angeles (LAX) to New York (JFK) on September 20, 2026, with their lap infant, baby Smith (DOB 05JUL26), who travels on John's lap. Book them on the 8:33 AM departure. John would like a bassinet request noted, and Jane wants a vegetarian meal. Jane's frequent flyer number is AA1234567, Gold tier. Assign both adults window and aisle seats in row 14 of your choice (skip any seat the map shows as occupied). Their travel agency, ACME Corp, has a negotiated fare code (ACME01) — apply it when pricing. Payment is by Visa, card number 4111111111111111, expiring 09/28.

  • Sell 2 seats on the correct flight, then add both names (with the infant attached to John's entry).
  • Add the bassinet SSR for passenger 1, the meal SSR for passenger 2, and Jane's frequent flyer number tied to her passenger number.
  • Pull up the seat map and assign two seats in row 14.
  • Price with the corporate code, then complete the rest of the PNR and issue tickets for all 3 (2 adults + 1 infant).

New ground covered: multiple passengers + lap infant, SSRs, FQTV tied to a passenger with a loyalty tier, seat maps and assignment, corporate fare codes, credit card form of payment.

3

A connecting itinerary — and a schedule change

Intermediate

Jane Doe needs to fly DFW → ORD on August 15, 2026 — the same route as Scenario 1 — but this time book her onto the connecting itinerary that routes through Fez (FAZ), in Y class, 1 seat. Price, complete, and save the PNR, then issue her ticket. Once you've saved it, see what the PNR display is telling you — something about this itinerary changed after booking. Handle it the way the on-screen instruction says to, then try to cancel just the first leg of her connection on its own. Note what happens, then cancel the whole connection correctly.

  • Pull up the same availability display as Scenario 1 and identify the two lines that connect through FAZ.
  • Sell both legs together as one connection entry (1 seat, Y class both legs).
  • Complete and save the PNR as usual, then ticket it.
  • Read the alert on the redisplayed PNR and resolve it before moving on.
  • Attempt to cancel only the first segment — read the error.
  • Cancel both segments together, the way the error message tells you to.

New ground covered: selling a connection (married segments), reading a schedule-change alert, re-pricing/reissuing after a schedule change, why a married pair can't be cancelled independently.

4

Splitting a party

Intermediate+

The White family — Robert, Susan, and Emily White — are booked JFK → LHR on October 10, 2026, on the 9:17 AM departure, 3 seats in Y class. Build, price, complete, save, and ticket this PNR for all three. Then: Emily has decided to fly out a day later than her parents on a separate ticket (in this exercise, just split her into her own PNR — don't worry about actually changing her date). Split her out, and get both resulting PNRs — the parents' and Emily's — back to a fully re-priced, re-ticketed state.

  • Sell 3 seats on the correct flight and add all three passengers in one name entry.
  • Price, complete, save, and ticket the PNR for all three.
  • Divide Emily out into a new PNR.
  • Notice what the PNR display tells you about pricing/tickets on the original — fix it and re-save/re-ticket.
  • Retrieve Emily's new PNR by its locator and get it re-priced, saved, and reticketed too. (Check what's already carried over from the original before re-entering anything.)

New ground covered: multi-passenger name entries, PNR divide (SP), what carries over vs. what needs redoing on each side of a split, retrieving a PNR by locator.

5

Booking a group

Advanced

A tour operator, Global Tours, needs to hold 12 seats on UA flight 1900, Y class, DFW → ORD on November 15, 2026 — names aren't finalized yet. This is more space than any single availability line will show, so you'll need to book it directly by flight number rather than off a display. Build the PNR with placeholder names, price it, add contact/received-from/ticketing/payment, and try to save it — read what the terminal tells you is still missing for a group this size, and fix it. Once it's saved, one passenger's name has come in: Carlos Diaz. Replace the first placeholder with his real name and re-save.

  • Long-sell 12 seats directly on UA1900 (Y class, 15NOV, DFW-ORD) — no availability display needed for this one.
  • Add 12 placeholder passengers in a single name entry.
  • Price the itinerary, then add phone, received-from, ticketing arrangement, and form of payment (cash is fine).
  • Try to end-transact. Read the error and resolve it with the correct entry.
  • Save the PNR.
  • Cancel the first placeholder passenger and add Carlos Diaz's real name in his place, then re-save.

New ground covered: long/direct sell for space beyond what a display can offer, group placeholder names (-{N}TBA/TBA), the 10+ passenger group-deposit gate on end transaction (3DEPS), finalizing a placeholder passenger.

Solutions

Full command streams, in order, with a line-by-line explanation. Every flight, fare, and seat map below is exactly what you'll see if you enter the same route/date/flight details from each scenario — nothing here is randomized except the record locator and generated ticket numbers, which will differ each time you run it yourself.

1

A simple one-way ticket

SI A15AUGDFWORD 01Y1 -SMITH/JOHN MR WP 9214555-1234-A 6JSMITH 7TAW/ FPCASH ER TKTT
  1. A15AUGDFWORD — line 1 (AK2265, 5:39A departure) is the earliest flight on this display and always the first line shown for any route/date, so it's a safe "earliest flight" pick without needing to scan times.
  2. 01Y1 — sells 1 seat, Y class, from line 1.
  3. -SMITH/JOHN MR — adds the one passenger the single seat allows.
  4. WP — prices the itinerary (this flight/date has no schedule change on file, so nothing to resolve later).
  5. 9214555-1234-A / 6JSMITH / 7TAW/ / FPCASH — the remaining four fields end transaction always requires.
  6. ER — saves the PNR and returns a record locator.
  7. TKTT — issues one ticket number for SMITH/JOHN MR.
2

A family with an infant and special requests

SI A20SEPLAXJFK 03Y2 -SMITH/JOHN MR(INFSMITH/BABY/05JUL26) -SMITH/JANE MRS 3BSCT-1 3VGML-2 3FQTVAA1234567-2/GLD 41 41-14A/1 41-14G/2 WP/ACME01 9213555-2000-A 6JDOE 7TAW/ FPCCVI4111111111111111/0928 ER TKTT
  1. A20SEPLAXJFK — line 3 (JQ772) departs 8:33A, matching the brief.
  2. 03Y2 — sells 2 Y-class seats for the two adults (the infant doesn't count against this).
  3. Two separate - entries since the adults don't share a surname format constraint here — John's entry carries the infant suffix.
  4. 3BSCT-1 / 3VGML-2 — SSRs tied to passenger 1 (John) and passenger 2 (Jane) respectively by their pax number in the name list.
  5. 3FQTVAA1234567-2/GLD — frequent flyer number tied to passenger 2 (Jane), with a Gold tier suffix. The passenger number and tier are each optional and independent of each other — -2 and /GLD can be used together, alone, or dropped entirely for a PNR-level FQTV not attributed to anyone specific.
  6. 41 displays the seat map for segment 1 (a 777, 3-3-3 layout) — row 14 seats B/C/D/E/F/H are occupied on this generated map, so 14A and 14G are the two open seats actually assignable in that row. With 2 passengers on the PNR, the passenger number is required on assignment (omitting it — as with a solo passenger — gets rejected: MULTIPLE PASSENGERS ON FILE - SPECIFY PASSENGER NUMBER) — 41-14A/1 ties 14A to John, 41-14G/2 ties 14G to Jane. This attribution is what would let either seat follow its passenger through a later SP divide (see Scenario 4) instead of being dropped as unattributed.
  7. WP/ACME01 — prices with the corporate discount code applied.
  8. FPCCVI4111111111111111/0928 — Visa, masked to XXXXXXXXXXXX1111 once stored.
  9. ER then TKTT — saves and issues 3 ticket numbers (John, Jane, and the infant).
3

A connecting itinerary — and a schedule change

SI A15AUGDFWORD 01Y7Y8 -DOE/JANE MS WP 9214555-9999-A 6JDOE 7TAW/ FPCASH ER WP TKTT X2 X2,3
  1. Same A15AUGDFWORD display as Scenario 1 — lines 7 (ET1276, DFW→FAZ) and 8 (VA176, FAZ→ORD) are this route/date's generated connection: line 7's destination (FAZ) matches line 8's origin, with a workable 47-minute connection.
  2. 01Y7Y8 — 1 seat, Y class on both legs. Both segments come back tagged MARRIED TO SEG2/MARRIED TO SEG1.
  3. WP prices both legs together as one itinerary; the rest of the fields and ER save it as usual.
  4. On redisplay after ER, a ** SCHEDULE CHANGE ON FILE ** notice appears against segment 1 (ET1276) — this flight/date combination always rolls a schedule change, so you'll see it every time. The notice tells you to re-price and reissue: a second WP clears it, and TKTT issues the ticket against the corrected time.
  5. X2 (cancelling just segment 1 alone) is rejected: UNABLE TO CANCEL - ELEMENT 2 IS MARRIED TO ELEMENT 3 - CANCEL BOTH TOGETHER (X2,3) — exactly the entry the error message names.
  6. X2,3 cancels both legs together, which succeeds.
4

Splitting a party

SI A10OCTJFKLHR 03Y3 -3WHITE/ROBERT MR/SUSAN MRS/EMILY MISS WP 9212555-4400-A 6RWHITE 7TAW/ FPCASH ER TKTT SP3 WP ER TKTT *{NEW LOCATOR SHOWN BY SP3} WP ER TKTT
  1. A10OCTJFKLHR — line 3 (BA542) departs 9:17A, matching the brief.
  2. 03Y3 then -3WHITE/... — one sell for all 3 seats, one name entry for all 3 passengers (shared surname, three given-name/title pairs).
  3. Price, complete, save, and ticket as usual — 3 ticket numbers issued.
  4. SP3 — divides passenger 3 (Emily, the last name added) out. The response names the new PNR's record locator and confirms the original's is unchanged but needs re-saving. It also invalidates pricing/tickets on the original PNR (party size dropped from 3 to 2), which is why it needs another WP/ER/TKTT before it's clean again.
  5. Retrieving Emily's new PNR (*{locator}) shows her contact info, received-from, form of payment, and ticketing arrangement were all copied over automatically by the divide — only pricing and tickets need redoing on her side too, since her party size (now 1) also differs from the original 3. No need to re-enter phone/RF/FOP/ticketing on her PNR.
5

Booking a group

SI 0UA1900Y15NOVDFWORDNN12 -12TBA/TBA WP 9214555-7000-A 6GTOURS 7TAW/ FPCASH ER 3DEPS ER X1 -DIAZ/CARLOS MR ER
  1. 0UA1900Y15NOVDFWORDNN12 — a long/direct sell: airline UA, flight 1900, Y class, 15NOV, DFW→ORD, status NN, 12 seats. This bypasses the availability display entirely, which is the only way to book more seats than any one display line offers.
  2. -12TBA/TBA — the group-placeholder shorthand: 12 identical, unnumbered TBA/TBA names in one entry, matching the real group-booking convention. Party size (12) matches the 12 seats just sold.
  3. WP and the phone/RF/ticketing/FOP entries complete every field the base end transaction rule requires.
  4. The first ER is rejected: PNR INCOMPLETE - GROUP DEPOSIT REQUIRED FOR 10+ PASSENGERS (ENTRY: 3DEPS) — this only appears once every other field is already satisfied, since it's a bespoke check layered on top of (not a replacement for) the standard completeness rules.
  5. 3DEPS — records the group deposit as an SSR (DEPS/"GROUP DEPOSIT RECEIVED"). The second ER now succeeds and returns a record locator.
  6. X1 cancels the first NM element (the first TBA/TBA placeholder); -DIAZ/CARLOS MR adds his real name in an ordinary name entry — no dedicated "replace placeholder" command exists, since cancel-then-add already does the job. His name is appended as the new last name in the list (still 12 total).
  7. The final ER re-saves under the same record locator, since only a name changed — no itinerary/pricing impact.
